# RES Exam Bank

## Founder Business Plan v1

## 1. Executive Summary

RES Exam Bank is a focused online revision platform for people preparing for the Singapore Real Estate Salesperson exam. It is designed to help learners practice more consistently, identify weak areas faster, and build confidence through active revision instead of passive note-reading.

The product will start as a web-based exam-prep tool with:

- Paper 1 and Paper 2 question banks
- topic-based practice
- mock exams
- answer explanations
- basic progress tracking

The product tone should feel credible, learner-friendly, and slightly energizing. It should not feel like a generic quiz app or a childish gamified product. The core promise is simple:

`Help Singapore RES candidates revise smarter, practice consistently, and pass with more confidence.`

This is intended as a real income-generating product, not just a portfolio project.

---

## 2. Founder Goal

The business goal is to build a niche digital product that can become a primary income source by serving a recurring and clearly defined exam-preparation market in Singapore.

The immediate founder objective is:

- launch a focused MVP quickly
- validate willingness to pay
- acquire first real users
- improve retention and conversion based on usage

The near-term success target is not “build everything.” It is:

`Get a small number of real users to use the product repeatedly and pay for it.`

---

## 3. Problem

Singapore RES candidates often study through a messy mix of:

- PDFs
- tuition notes
- Telegram or WhatsApp groups
- old question compilations
- random online summaries

This creates several problems:

- revision is passive instead of active
- learners do not know what they are weak in
- study quality depends too much on discipline
- there is little structured feedback
- practice is inconsistent
- confidence is often based on guesswork

Working adults and career switchers face an additional problem: they do not have large blocks of uninterrupted study time, so they need short, structured, high-value practice sessions.

The current market has tuition, notes, and informal community help, but there is room for a focused digital revision system that is easier to use, more affordable, and available on demand.

---

## 4. Product Vision

RES Exam Bank should become the practical revision companion for Singapore RES candidates.

It should be known for:

- clear structure
- useful practice
- strong explanations
- visible progress
- low-friction revision

The long-term vision is to become a trusted niche exam-prep brand in the Singapore real estate learning ecosystem.

The short-term product vision is much narrower:

`A clean, mobile-friendly practice platform for Paper 1 and Paper 2 revision.`

---

## 5. Target Market

### Broad Audience

Anyone preparing for the Singapore RES exam.

### Priority User Segments

These should be treated as the first real target users:

- first-time candidates
- working adults
- career switchers
- repeat candidates who need targeted revision

### First Buyer Focus

For MVP, the best initial buyer is likely:

`Working adults and serious first-time candidates who want structured self-practice without depending fully on tuition.`

Why this segment matters:

- they value time efficiency
- they are more likely to pay for convenience
- they need flexible access
- they are motivated by a clear pass outcome

## 6. Value Proposition

RES Exam Bank offers a better revision experience than static notes by making revision:

- active
- measurable
- structured
- repeatable

Compared with tuition-only prep, it is:

- cheaper
- more flexible
- available anytime
- better for repeated practice

Compared with free notes or group chats, it is:

- more organized
- easier to trust
- easier to track
- more focused on exam execution

Core value proposition:

`A focused RES revision system that helps candidates practice by topic, measure progress, and improve confidence before the exam.`

## 8. MVP Scope

The MVP must stay lean.

### MVP Features

- user sign-up and login
- Paper 1 question bank
- Paper 2 question bank
- topic filters
- quiz mode
- timed mock exam mode
- answer explanations
- score summary after each session
- basic progress tracking by topic and paper
- admin question management

### Explicitly Not MVP

Do not build these first:

- leaderboards
- community discussion
- AI explanations
- native mobile apps
- advanced analytics dashboards
- tutor marketplace
- agency admin portals
- large gamification systems

### Why the MVP Must Stay Lean

The first version exists to answer:

- Will candidates actually use it?
- Will they come back?
- Will they pay?

Anything that does not help answer those questions should be delayed.

## 9. User Experience Strategy

The platform should be slightly fun and engaging, but still exam-focused.

### UX Principles

- mobile-friendly first
- fast to enter a quiz
- minimal friction
- visible progress
- clean hierarchy
- no clutter

### Engagement Strategy

Use lightweight motivation only:

- practice streaks
- topic completion indicators
- mock exam milestones
- encouraging feedback
- “continue where you left off”

### Core UX Goal

Users should always know:

- what to practice next
- how they are doing
- where they are weak
- whether they are improving

## 10. Business Model

### Recommended Starting Model

Start with:

- free limited access
- paid exam-prep package

### Suggested Structure

Free plan:

- limited sample questions
- limited topic access
- limited mock exam preview

Paid plan:

- full Paper 1 and Paper 2 access
- full mock exams
- full explanations
- progress tracking

### Best Early Commercial Option

Use a:

`90-day or 120-day exam access package`

This matches actual exam-prep behavior better than a long open-ended subscription.

### Why This Model Fits

- simple to explain
- matches exam timelines
- easier to convert than a pure subscription
- feels outcome-based

## 15. Technology Plan

The build should match your chosen stack and stay practical.

### Recommended Stack

- Frontend: React
- Backend: Spring Boot
- Database: MySQL
- Authentication: JWT + Spring Security

### Delivery Format

- web-first
- mobile-responsive
- simple admin panel

### Why This Stack Works

- credible enterprise-style stack
- flexible enough for growth
- good for secure user accounts and structured data
- suitable for question banks, progress tracking, and admin management

### Important Rule

Do not let architecture become a hiding place for avoiding launch.

The product only matters if users actually use it.

---

## 16. Operations Plan

### Content Operations

Question quality is one of the biggest business risks and one of the biggest product strengths.

Questions must be:

- categorized correctly
- syllabus-aligned
- clearly worded
- reviewed
- updated when necessary

### Core Operational Needs

- question creation process
- review and editing process
- topic tagging
- answer explanation writing
- issue reporting from users

### Lean Team Model

At first, this can operate with:

- founder/product lead
- content reviewer or subject-matter support
- developer execution
- part-time design support if needed

## 17. Pricing Strategy

### Early Recommendation

Keep pricing simple.

Possible launch options:

- 90-day access package
- 120-day access package
- beta launch discount

### Positioning of Price

The price should feel:

- affordable relative to tuition
- high enough to imply quality
- low enough for trial purchase

### Founder Pricing Principle

Price for seriousness, not for maximum volume at the start.

People preparing for an exam are paying for:

- convenience
- confidence
- structure
- time savings

## 18. Financial Framework

Use this as a planning model, not a promise.

### Key Variables

- monthly traffic
- signup rate
- active learner rate
- paid conversion rate
- package price

### Conservative Early Planning Scenario

- 500 to 1,500 monthly targeted visitors
- 5% to 10% signup rate
- 3% to 6% paid conversion from signups
- single paid package

### Base Planning Scenario

- stronger traffic through content and communities
- better conversion from trust and proof
- repeat package sales during exam cycles

### Founder Reminder

Distribution matters more than elegant projections.

Do not anchor emotionally to optimistic numbers before distribution is proven.

## 19. Major Risks

### Risk 1: Low Willingness to Pay

Mitigation:

- strong free sample access
- visible value before paywall
- clear exam relevance

### Risk 2: Weak Content Quality

Mitigation:

- rigorous review
- smaller high-quality launch bank first
- feedback flagging

### Risk 3: Overbuilding

Mitigation:

- strict MVP
- short build cycles
- launch before feature sprawl

### Risk 4: User Acquisition Difficulty

Mitigation:

- early channel testing
- content marketing
- partnerships
- referrals

### Risk 5: Founder Confusion Across Projects

Mitigation:

- keep naming consistent as `RES Exam Bank`
- keep product repo separate from portfolio framing
- maintain one main roadmap document

## 23. Strategic Rules for the Founder

- Keep the product focused on passing the RES exam.
- Do not mix this product with unrelated portfolio positioning.
- Do not add “nice-to-have” features before users prove the core is useful.
- Question quality matters more than flashy UI.
- Explanations matter more than branding polish.
- Distribution matters more than overthinking architecture.
- A smaller product with paying users beats a larger unfinished platform.

---

## 24. Conclusion

RES Exam Bank has real potential as a focused niche digital business because it serves a clear exam-prep need with a product that can be delivered online, improved over time, and sold to a recurring stream of candidates.

The strongest path forward is not to build the biggest version first. It is to:

- launch a narrow but useful product
- validate user demand
- improve based on real behavior
- build trust through quality
- convert revision usefulness into paid value

The immediate mission is simple:

`Build the smallest credible RES revision platform that users will actually return to and pay for.`
